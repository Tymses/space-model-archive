ABOUT SPACEMESHER

From space data to a physical model.

A compact guide to what this archive contains, where the models come from and how to turn a digital model into a print.

What is SPACEMESHER?

SPACEMESHER is an index for discovering space-related 3D geometry: spacecraft, planetary landscapes, asteroids, comets, moons and height maps. It brings together files and links from scientific institutions, mission teams and independent projects.

The archive helps you find a model, inspect its geometry, understand where it came from and—where reliable measurements exist—compare its scale with the real object. Original files remain with their providers whenever possible; SPACEMESHER links to them rather than claiming ownership.

Important: a catalogue entry is not always a downloadable model. The separate sections tell you whether an entry is a verified file, a page to investigate, a source or a generator.

Where do the models come from?

Sources include NASA and JPL, planetary data archives, research catalogues, ESA and independent 3D projects. Each entry links back to its original provider so you can check the dataset, licence, credits and available formats.

  • Downloadable models: a direct model file has been identified and checked.
  • Remote model candidates: SPACEMESHER opens the original object page and checks whether usable OBJ/PLY files are available. Only files that pass the check are promoted to downloads.
  • Sources: catalogues, scientific datasets and viewers. They may contain additional files, but are not necessarily direct downloads.

Always read the original provider's licence and attribution terms before reusing or redistributing a model. A source link is not itself a licence grant.

What are generators?

A generator creates geometry from selected data and settings instead of offering one fixed model file. For example, terrain tools can let you choose a planet, region, map resolution, vertical relief and tile size, then export a mesh for printing.

NASA Solar System Treks and Terrain Print are examples. Choose the world or terrain area in the external tool, configure the output and export the generated 3D file. The generator runs on its own provider's website; SPACEMESHER is the directory, not the generator itself.

The 3D-printing workflow

  1. Design / source dataCreate geometry in CAD or obtain a scientific model or terrain export.
  2. 3D fileSave or download a format such as STL, 3MF, OBJ, PLY, GLB or STEP. Not every format is accepted by every slicer.
  3. SlicerOpen the model in software such as Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer. Orient it, choose material, layer height, supports and print settings.
  4. G-codeThe slicer converts the prepared model and settings into printer instructions (usually G-code).
  5. TransferSend the G-code over the network or copy it to a memory card / USB drive, depending on the printer.
  6. PrinterThe printer reads the instructions and builds the object layer by layer.

SPACEMESHER does not include a slicer and does not send G-code to your printer. Its Scaling workspace previews geometry, calculates scale and checks a bounding box against a build volume. This is not a slicing simulation and cannot guarantee a successful print.

Tip: GLB/glTF normally stores dimensions in metres. STL, OBJ and PLY may not specify units, so check the source documentation or choose the correct file unit yourself.

What this assessment can and cannot tell you

  • Measured: model bounding dimensions, six axis-aligned 90° orientations, scale conversion and remaining space in the selected build volume.
  • Not measured: wall thickness, minimum feature size, mesh defects, overhangs, support demand, bed adhesion, material behaviour, thermal warping or slicer collision checks.
  • Interpretation: the displayed fit index is a geometric clearance/scale-down indicator, not a probability of a successful print or a quality score.
  • Recommendation: inspect the mesh in your slicer, orient it for strength and support reduction, then run the slicer's own plate and collision checks before printing.
